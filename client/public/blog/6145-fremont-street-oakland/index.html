The list price said $1,098,000. Nobody who'd done their homework believed it. Including us.

6145 Fremont Street is a century-old North Oakland home in the Golden Gate area, close enough to Emeryville that you can hear the bakery calling, but squarely inside the City of Oakland. Four bedrooms, three and a half baths, a finished upper level that turns "where do the guests sleep?" into a non-question, and the kind of 1912 bones that newer construction keeps trying to imitate.

The List Price Was an Invitation, Not a Valuation

When a home is priced low to draw a crowd, the worst thing a buyer can do is anchor on the asking number. It's a starting gun, not a target. So before offer day, we threw the list price out and priced the house the way an appraiser and a disciplined investor would: off the recent North Oakland sales, adjusted for size, condition, and that extra living space upstairs.

That gave my buyers a number they actually believed in, which matters more than people think. A buyer who knows their walk-away price writes a confident offer. A buyer guessing at it writes a nervous one, and sellers can smell the difference.

Read Everything. Then Read It Again.

Old houses come with stories, and the disclosure package is where they're told. We went through every document before writing a word of the offer: inspections, the pest report, roof and sewer paperwork, the hazard disclosures, and what the seller had already repaired. When you know exactly what you're buying, you can offer clean instead of padding the contract with "just in case" contingencies. Clean wins.

Fast, Certain, Done

Winning the offer is half the job. The other half is closing on the timeline you promised. My buyers had financing lined up and ready to lock, the escrow file opened the next morning, and everyone on the team worked to one rule: time is of the essence. Fifteen days from acceptance to keys. Final price: $1,450,000.
