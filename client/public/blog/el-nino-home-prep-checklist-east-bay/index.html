Homeowner Guide · Winter 2026–27

El Niño is coming. Here's your East Bay home prep checklist.

It's still dry out. That's exactly the point — the best time to fix a leak is when it isn't leaking.

By Patrick MacCartee · The Grubb Company · DRE# 02142693

What NOAA is actually saying

This isn't a "maybe it'll rain" year. NOAA's Climate Prediction Center has an El Niño Advisory in effect, and its October update reads less like a forecast and more like a weather bulletin with a raised eyebrow:

>83%chance of a strong-to-very-strong El Niño through Jan–Mar 2027
83%chance of a record-strength event in Oct–Dec (stronger than anything since 1950)
Through Dechow long NOAA expects it to keep strengthening

Source: NOAA Climate Prediction Center, ENSO Diagnostic Discussion, October 8, 2026. Next update scheduled November 12, 2026. A strong El Niño tilts the odds toward a wet California winter; it doesn't guarantee one.

Translation for anyone who owns a house in Oakland, Berkeley, Piedmont, Alameda or anywhere with a hill behind it: plan for the wet winter, and be pleasantly surprised if it doesn't show up. The cost of prepping is a Saturday and a few service calls. The cost of not prepping is a soggy crawlspace, a slope that's decided to relocate, and a roofer who can see you "after the holidays."

The checklist tells you what to fix. The hard part, every single fall, is finding someone good who can come out before it rains.

The real bottleneck: getting someone out there

Here's the part no checklist mentions. In a big storm year, every good roofer, arborist and drainage contractor in the East Bay is booked solid by the second atmospheric river. The ones still answering their phones in January are usually still answering for a reason.

After years of prepping homes for market — and fixing what inspections turn up — I keep a short list of the people I'd actually send to my own house. Roofers, gutter crews, arborists, drainage and grading, structural engineers, foundation, HVAC, handymen, gardeners and more. Nearly 100 of them, vetted on real jobs, not star ratings.
