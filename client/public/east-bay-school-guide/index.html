How East Bay Districts Actually Assign Schools

Every district on this map assigns students differently — and the differences matter more than the ratings do. A 9-rated school you can't get into is worth less than a 7 you're guaranteed. Here's how each one actually works.

Oakland Unified assigns by boundary, then runs a lottery

Your address puts you in an attendance zone, and that zone gives you priority — but priority is not placement. Siblings of enrolled students are seated first, and popular schools fill. Families zoned to a lower-rated high school frequently pursue a lottery transfer, so the assigned school often isn't the destination. Buy the zone for the odds, not for the certainty.

Berkeley Unified doesn't assign by address at all

Berkeley runs a controlled-choice system. Your address places you in one of three zones — Northwest, Central, or Southeast — and you rank your preferences among the schools in that zone. A lottery weighted for socioeconomic diversity makes the assignment. You cannot buy a specific Berkeley elementary school by buying a specific block; two identical houses across the street from each other can send their kids to different schools. Middle school follows the same three-zone logic. High school is the one certainty: every Berkeley public high school student attends Berkeley High.

Piedmont is its own city and its own district

Piedmont is geographically surrounded by Oakland but entirely separate from Oakland Unified — which regularly confuses buyers in both directions. Only elementary school depends on your address; Beach, Havens, and Wildwood each have their own boundary. After that, everyone attends Piedmont Middle and Piedmont High. There's no bad outcome to plan around, which is part of what the premium buys.

Alameda has two high school pipelines

Alameda Unified uses neighborhood boundaries with district-wide choice programs layered on top. The part buyers miss: the island splits. Most of the main island feeds Alameda High, while the west end feeds Encinal Jr/Sr High, which serves grades 6–12. Which side of Alameda you buy on decides which pipeline you're in.

Albany is one square mile with one of everything

Three elementary schools — Cornell, Marin, and Ocean View — then everyone converges. Albany Middle is the district's only middle school and Albany High its only comprehensive high, so buying inside the city limits settles grades 6 through 12 entirely.

San Leandro varies until high school

Elementary and middle assignments follow neighborhood boundaries, but there's a single comprehensive high school in the district. The 9–12 question is answered the moment you buy in the city.

Lamorinda is two districts stacked on top of each other

Orinda, Lafayette, and Moraga each run their own K–8 elementary district. After eighth grade, students move into the Acalanes Union High School District — a separate district entirely. The mistake that costs people: assuming the city name determines the high school. Orinda addresses don't all feed Miramonte; depending on where you are, you may be assigned to Campolindo in Moraga. Walnut Creek splits further still, with part of the city feeding Las Lomas in the Acalanes district and part feeding Mt. Diablo Unified. If you're buying for a specific high school, verify the address before you write the offer.

El Cerrito and Kensington are West Contra Costa Unified

Both are served by WCCUSD, which also covers Richmond, San Pablo, Pinole, and Hercules. Assignment is by neighborhood boundary with a transfer and choice process on top. Kensington is unincorporated — no city government — which surprises buyers who assume it's part of Berkeley or El Cerrito, and its school, service, and tax lines don't all follow the same boundaries.

Ratings are a starting point, not a conclusion

The 1–10 scores on this page compare schools within California using test scores, college readiness, academic progress, and equity measures. They're useful for orientation and misleading in isolation — a school's score reflects its student population as much as its teaching, and small schools swing hard year to year. Read them alongside the assignment system above. A guaranteed 7 and a lottery 9 are not the same asset.
