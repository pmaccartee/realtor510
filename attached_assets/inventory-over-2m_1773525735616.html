How is inventory trending for homes over $2 million?

Tight. That's the short answer — and it has been for long enough now that it's structural, not cyclical.

The supply picture in 2025–2026

In Piedmont, Crocker Highlands, Trestle Glen, and the premium Berkeley Hills corridors, the number of homes that come to market above $2M in any given year is small by any measure. Piedmont saw roughly 15 sales above $3M in 2025. Crocker Highlands turns over perhaps 30 to 40 homes annually across all price points. These are not markets with deep benches of available inventory — they are neighborhoods where buyers often wait months for the right home to appear.

That scarcity has a predictable effect on price. In Piedmont's $3M tier, the median sold price in 2025 was $318,500 above the median list price. Homes closed in 13 days. At the $5–6.5M tier, the over-ask spread widened to 16.4%. These are not soft markets hedging toward buyers — they are seller's markets at every tier below $10M.

Why inventory stays low

Several forces are keeping supply constrained. Long-term owners in these neighborhoods — particularly those who bought 15 to 30 years ago — are sitting on substantial equity but face significant capital gains exposure if they sell. Prop 19 has helped some transfer their tax base to a replacement home, but the calculus still keeps many owners in place longer than they might otherwise choose.

At the same time, the lock-in effect of sub-3% mortgages from 2020 and 2021 continues to suppress move-up activity. Owners who refinanced at 2.75% have little financial incentive to trade into a new mortgage at current rates, even if their housing needs have changed.

What it means for buyers

At this tier, patience is a liability. Well-priced homes in desirable neighborhoods move in days, not weeks. Buyers who are not pre-approved, not aligned on decision-making, and not working with an agent who has advance knowledge of coming inventory will consistently find themselves a step behind.

The buyers who succeed in this market are the ones who are genuinely ready — financially, emotionally, and logistically — before the right home appears. That preparation takes weeks, not days. Start it early.

What it means for sellers

Scarcity is working in your favor, but only if the home is well-presented and correctly priced. The data on 2025 Piedmont sales is instructive: homes that were priced to invite competition closed significantly over ask. Homes that were priced aspirationally sat — the $10M+ tier averaged 245 days on market. The market rewards precision. It does not reward optimism unmoored from comps.
